Nataraja and Nausea

Shiva dances. Feet of the wind thundering onto icy ground, steps in a circle, pirouette and turn. His eyes are closed and his neck turned up. He sees not whether he has run out of stage. He is All. No Other exists. Shiva is ego-manifest.

To feel his heedless abandon and his freedom for a moment, I attempt an approximation of Shiva. Instead I find inside a subtle resentment of the Other. My stage is cut up and bounded at every turn. Shiva has it easier than me.

To be sure, it is not the external resistance which carves out my world that bothers me. I accept the dues I must pay for being in a world of beings. Once the canals are dug in however, I simply wish to flow through them freely. The stage I speak of is internal. I accept the Other as such but wish to be free of the part of me that is plagued by its unreadable face.

At the notion of being twisted and tossed inside my own mind at the whim of the Other, I am overcome with disgust. Who is bigger than me in my own mind? Who are any of you to submit me into movement or binding in my own world? Disgust, it is disgust that coats every morsel of food down my throat. I cannot bear the shame of being shackled in my backyard.

Disgust forces my hand the way no fact or sentence could. It is pre-verbal and instinctive, just as a child vomits out food he cannot bear. I wipe the vomit from my face and stand up off the ground. My chest feels lighter and my breath returns to me. Having been disgusted at being moved by another's will, like a courtesan and a puppet, my mind goes silent. For a moment, I am alone in my mind and my body and no gaze persists in my awareness. It is as if no Other exists, for this moment. It is as if I am Shiva, for this moment.
